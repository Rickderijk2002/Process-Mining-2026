This assignment focuses on implements the deviation analysis component of this method. Throughput time is defined as the elapsed time between the first and last event of a trace, following the KPI definition used in the UWV case study of Dees et al. [@Dees2017].

As a concrete use case, the loan application process of a Dutch financial institution from the BPI Challenge 2017 [@vanDongen2017BPIC] is taken, restricted to the denied applications. These applications take between less than a day and about 260 days from the first to the last event. A process owner may ask which deviations from the process go together with slow denials, and whether the process should allow or forbid them. ## Step 4: Rules and trace subsets

134 to 122 words

The fourth step reads the fitted tree as rules. Every path from the root to a leaf is a conjunction of threshold conditions on move counts, and each leaf is one rule. The counts are integers, so a condition such as `log_move_X <= 0.5` means that activity X never occurs as a log move, and `log_move_X > 0.5` means that it occurs at least once. A rule predicts the class that the tree assigns to its leaf. For each rule, the artefact lists the identifiers of all traces that satisfy its conditions, including those whose actual class differs from the predicted one. Dees et al. keep only correctly classified traces in a cluster [@Dees2017]. The rules and trace lists cover all 3,093 traces, whereas precision and recall are computed on the hold-out set only.

## Data and evaluation design

The brief asks how well the approach generates the optimal alignments and how well it finds the right classification rules. We therefore evaluate in two ways. The correctness of the alignments and of the rules is tested on two small artificial logs against hand-built gold standards (@sec-gold). On the real log no gold standard for the rules exists, so we measure how well the tree separates slow from fast traces on a stratified hold-out of 25 percent of the traces, with the precision, recall and F1 of the slow class. ## Gold standards for alignments and rules

The correctness of the alignments and the rules is tested against gold standards built by hand, because the traces of the real log are too long to align manually. Two small logs with their nets serve this purpose: a linear net with five traces, and a net with six traces that adds a parallel block and an exclusive choice. The correct move counts of every trace were derived by hand before the artefact was run. For the rules, the throughput times of selected traces were planted so that one deviation separates slow from fast traces, and the recovered trace sets are compared with the planted ones. Both toys reproduce the hand-derived move counts exactly (5 of 5 and 6 of 6 traces). Counting every move of the gold standard as one item, the aligner finds all gold moves and adds none, so the precision and the recall of the alignments are 1.0 on both toys. Both planted rules are recovered with precision and recall of 1.0.
